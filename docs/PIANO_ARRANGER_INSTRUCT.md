# Piano Arranger Long-Run Instructions

Last updated: 2026-06-06

This document is the working memory for the DGGR piano-arrangement goal. Read it first after any context compaction, resume, or new session. It is written for Codex agents and for Ahmed. Its job is to keep the work focused on the real intention: train a new model that can take any song and produce a full, powerful piano arrangement, not merely repaint audio with a piano-like timbre.

- Model architecture comparison now exists:
  - `PianoRollModelConfig.architecture` supports `conv1d`, `key_conditioned`, `chroma_key_conditioned`, `harmony_conditioned`, and `musical_plan_conditioned`.
  - `key_conditioned` uses learned per-key embeddings combined with projected temporal features before onset/frame/velocity heads.
  - `chroma_key_conditioned` additionally injects source chroma for each piano key's pitch class into the per-key heads. This is the first architecture targeted directly at the paired path's active-harmony failure.
  - `harmony_conditioned` predicts a supervised 12-channel harmonic plan, injects that predicted pitch-class plan into each piano key head, and can train the plan with `--harmonic-plan-loss-weight`.
  - `musical_plan_conditioned` keeps source-chroma plus harmonic-plan conditioning and also injects predicted chord, bass/root-proxy, voicing-plan, event, pitch-class-onset, role/fullness, melody/top-line, joint texture-role, section-role, explicit arranger-state, split bass-continuity, split body/melody, and section-diversity signals into each key's note heads. This is the first architecture where the new musical-plan representation changes note generation, not only auxiliary loss or decode ranking.
  - All current architectures now also predict explicit musical-plan heads: `chord_logits` for 12 pitch classes plus no-chord, `bass_logits` for lowest active pitch-class plus no-bass, and `voicing` for note count, span, center, and high-register fraction curves. They also predict `role [5,T]` for bass activity, chord-frame activity, melody-register activity, normalized polyphony, and active-frame velocity weight; `melody [4,T]` for high activity, upper-register activity, top pitch, and top velocity; `texture_role [4,T]` for bass floor, chord body, inner motion, and top-line presence; `section_role [4,T]` for section-level bass coverage, chord body, melody presence, and fullness broadcast over local sections; `arranger_state [8,T]` for bass rhythm, bass sustain, chord body, inner motion, top line, section bass continuity, section fullness, and section-transition emphasis; `bass_continuity [4,T]` for bass rhythm, bass sustain, section bass continuity, and section transitions; `body_melody_state [6,T]` for chord body, inner motion, top line, high activity, section body, and section melody; and `section_diversity [4,T]` for section-level unique pitch coverage, pitch-class coverage, pitch range, and onset density. Train these with `--musical-plan-loss-weight`, `--role-plan-loss-weight`, `--melody-plan-loss-weight`, `--texture-role-plan-loss-weight`, `--section-role-plan-loss-weight`, `--arranger-state-plan-loss-weight`, `--bass-continuity-plan-loss-weight`, `--body-melody-state-plan-loss-weight`, `--body-melody-state-balance-loss-weight`, and `--section-diversity-plan-loss-weight`.
  - CLI args: `--model-architecture {conv1d,key_conditioned,chroma_key_conditioned,harmony_conditioned,musical_plan_conditioned}`, `--key-embed-dim`, `--harmonic-plan-loss-weight`, `--musical-plan-loss-weight`, and `--role-plan-loss-weight`.

## Current Goal

Build a DGGR-native song-to-piano arrangement system trained as its own model family.

The desired output is a musical piano performance:

- recognizably follows the source song's melody, harmony, energy, and section shape
- sounds like a full piano arrangement with bass, chords, inner rhythm, melody, dynamics, and sustain
- has volume, weight, and impact without flattening everything into clipping
- can create novel pianistic material when the source has sparse or non-piano instrumentation
- produces inspectable artifacts: MIDI or piano-roll, rendered WAV, metadata, metrics, and listening notes

## Avoid These Failure Modes

- Do not treat the existing real-music transfer checkpoint as the final solution. It can be a baseline, teacher, or temporary comparator, but the goal is a new model.
- Do not optimize only mel L1, style margin, or internal classifier confidence. Those can pass while the audio is boring, smeared, or not pianistic.
- Do not call a timbre-transfer output a piano arrangement unless there is explicit note/voicing/dynamics structure.
- Do not close the goal after a smoke run, a checkpoint, or one good sample.
- Do not start a long training run without a resumable output directory, logs, validation samples, and monitoring commands.
- Do not erase unrelated dirty work in this repository. The worktree has many pre-existing local changes.

## Architecture Direction

Use a hybrid symbolic-plus-audio system. This is the most direct route to "full sounding piano arrangement" because piano arrangement is about notes, voicings, register, density, pedal, and dynamics. Pure audio-to-audio transfer is too likely to smear source timbre and miss pianistic structure.

### Stage A: Source Analysis Encoder

Inputs: arbitrary song audio.

Representations to extract:

- BigVGAN mel, chroma, onset, beat, and Lab 1 content/style features already used by DGGR
- source loudness envelope and section-level energy
- melody or predominant pitch track when available
- bass/root estimate
- chord or harmonic context from chroma
- optional stem-aware features if source separation is available later

Output: a time-aligned conditioning sequence at a stable hop rate.

Implementation fit: extend `dggr/lab3_diffusion_data.py` conventions instead of inventing a separate cache format.

### Stage B: Piano Target Discovery and Data

Create a piano-focused training corpus, not just one discovered style label.

Initial data sources in this repo:

- existing real-music dataset and metadata discovery report
- discovered piano-like cluster: `style_00_midtempo_warm_tonal_loud_for_piano_now`
- any additional local piano/MIDI folders discovered later

Needed new artifacts:

- `data/piano_arranger_manifests/*.csv` for piano candidates and non-piano source candidates
- `saves2/piano_arranger/*_cache/` for cached features and targets
- a report showing how many usable piano tracks exist, why they were selected, and examples

The training target should become symbolic or semi-symbolic where possible:

- piano-roll onset/frame/velocity/sustain targets if transcription is used
- MIDI targets when a clean MIDI corpus is available
- audio piano mel targets only as a secondary decoder/rendering target

### Stage C: Arrangement Generator

Train a new model that maps source conditioning to piano performance representation.

Recommended first model:

- Transformer or U-Net/Transformer hybrid over fixed-time piano-roll frames
- outputs multi-head tensors:
  - note onset probability `[88, T]`
  - note sustain/frame probability `[88, T]`
  - velocity `[88, T]`
  - pedal or sustain control `[T]`
  - optional density/register controls `[T]`
- conditioning:
  - chroma, beat, onset, loudness, source mel summary
  - style controls for fullness, left-hand density, arpeggiation, octave doubling, and dynamics

Current smoke architectures:

- `conv1d`: temporal Conv1D backbone with direct 88-key 1x1 heads. Fast and compatible with older checkpoints, but pitch diversity collapses in tiny bootstrap runs.
- `key_conditioned`: same temporal backbone with learned key embeddings and per-key heads. It improves source/onset metrics in smoke tests but still does not solve pitch diversity alone.
- `chroma_key_conditioned`: key-conditioned heads plus source-chroma injection for each piano key's pitch class. This produced the first source-aware paired-smoke pass before target-MIDI gates were added.
- `harmony_conditioned`: chroma-key style conditioning plus a supervised predicted harmonic-plan head injected into per-key note heads. This is the current architecture pressure test for paired active harmony, especially with light learned harmonic-plan plus source-chroma guided decoding.
- `musical_plan_conditioned`: harmony-conditioned structure plus predicted chord, bass/root-proxy, and voicing plans injected into per-key note heads. This is the current model-side architecture pressure test.
- shared musical-plan heads: all architectures predict chord pitch-class set, bass/root-proxy pitch class, and voicing curves; train with `--musical-plan-loss-weight`, then optionally guide decode with `--chord-plan-guidance-weight`, `--bass-plan-guidance-weight`, and `--voicing-plan-guidance-weight`.
- pitch-class-onset plan head: all architectures predict a 12-channel event-level pitch-class onset plan. Train with `--pc-onset-plan-loss-weight`; optionally guide decode with `--pc-onset-plan-guidance-weight`. `--pc-onset-f1-loss-weight` adds a soft +/-1-frame F1 objective on the plan head. `--pc-onset-alignment-loss-weight` adds BCE/F1/distribution pressure from the actual note onset heads aggregated into pitch classes. Decode can also reserve candidates from pc-onset plan peaks with `--pc-onset-plan-reserve-threshold`, `--pc-onset-plan-reserve-max-per-frame`, and `--pc-onset-plan-reserve-min-note-score`. These are aimed at the strict target pitch-class-onset F1 gap, not at coarse chroma. Their first parser-pair comparisons did not beat the previous `0.3333` strict best, so future work should not treat any of them alone as the solution.

Next architecture pressure point: train the new plans on more than a one-pair smoke and make them improve event-level F1, not just coarse chroma. The latest pc-onset head plus event-distribution pressure and register-cap invariant improved strict pitch-class-onset F1 from `0.250` to `0.3333` without collapse, but still did not pass the `0.35` strict gate. The next model step should raise target pitch-class-onset F1 without reintroducing pitch-class collapse, likely through more paired data, longer training, and a stronger event/chord selection objective. The current parser-pair passes are not robust evidence until they hold across a real paired manifest and longer full-song validation.

### Stage D: Piano Renderer

First version should render MIDI/piano-roll to WAV with a stable local renderer.

Preferred rendering order:

1. MIDI output always.
2. WAV render with an available soundfont/synth if present.
3. If no synth is available, write MIDI plus a clear render-blocker report.
4. Later: train or integrate a neural piano renderer for richer tone.

Mastering is allowed, but it is not the core model. Mastering should add body and level after the arrangement exists.

### Stage E: Novelty Controls

Novelty should be explicit, not accidental.

Expose controls such as:

- `fullness`: chord density, octave doubling, and left-hand activity
- `melody_focus`: how strongly to preserve the source melodic contour
- `rhythmic_drive`: arpeggiation, repeated notes, stride, syncopation
- `harmonic_adventure`: passing chords, reharmonization, inner voices
- `register_width`: spread across keyboard
- `pedal_amount`: sustain and resonance

Validation must include at least two modes:

- faithful arrangement: source identity is obvious
- dramatic arrangement: source identity remains, but piano writing is more novel and powerful

## Current State And Next Step

The active goal is a DGGR-native song-to-piano arranger, not a wrapper around the old genre-transfer checkpoint. The current strongest learned body branch is the body-biased warm start from the role/texture checkpoint. It has the desired loud/full side, but it is high-register dominated on the Moonlight-style audit source and still fails `mid_harmony_underuse`.

Key evidence to preserve:

- `two_sources_warmstart_bodybiased_sectiondiv001_noguidance_sim8`: passed 1/2, mean notes `566`, bass/mid/high note fractions `0.085/0.191/0.725`, source onset corr `0.751`, chord `0.945`, bass `0.453`, melody `0.884`, fullness `0.988`, RMS `0.203`; failed Moonlight `mid_harmony_underuse`.
- `two_sources_warmstart_midselector_sectiondiv001_s1_sim8`: passed 1/2, mean notes `566`, bass/mid/high `0.084/0.191/0.726`, source onset corr `0.752`, chord `0.945`, bass `0.453`, melody `0.885`, fullness `0.988`, RMS `0.205`; Moonlight row was mid `0.108`, high `0.788`.
- `two_sources_warmstart_bodymelody_midbody_sectiondiv001_s1_sim8`: passed 1/2, mean notes `560.5`, bass/mid/high `0.091/0.201/0.707`, source onset corr `0.751`, chord `0.959`, bass `0.434`, melody `0.884`, fullness `0.993`, RMS `0.202`; Moonlight row improved only to mid `0.127`, high `0.766`, so it still failed `mid_harmony_underuse`.
- `two_sources_srcaware_24s_sectiondivrepair`: passed 2/2 with healthier bass/mid/high `0.194/0.443/0.363`, but it depends on post-hoc repair and is not proof the learned model can arrange this way.

Conclusion: sample-selector penalties are wired and fire, but selector-only warm starts did not alter deployed behavior. Existing `body_melody_state_balance_loss` moves the aggregate mid/high split slightly in the right direction but does not solve the hard Moonlight row. Do not spend more runs on the same selector-only recipe. The next model-side step should add stronger explicit mid/body supervision or candidate evidence:

1. Add a targeted mid-body floor / high-register dominance loss, likely extending the existing texture/body or `body_melody_state` balance path rather than creating a disconnected head.
2. Add source-audit reporting for any new mid-body objective, keeping `bass_note_fraction`, `mid_note_fraction`, and `high_note_fraction` in CSV and summary outputs.
3. Audit against the fixed two-source recipe before scaling. The minimum success bar is 2/2 without repair, Moonlight mid note fraction at least `0.20`, no overstacking under `--max-simultaneous-notes 8`, and fullness/RMS not collapsing below the body-biased branch.
