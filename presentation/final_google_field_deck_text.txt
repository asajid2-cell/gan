SLIDE 8: The Representation Problem || In order to do style transfer, there needs to exist a representation that is expressive enough for controllable transfer
SLIDE 9: Audio Representations || Audio can be represented as: /  / MIDI  /  / Spectrogram /  / Raw waveforms
SLIDE 10: Audio Representations || MIDI /  / A MIDI file stores information about notes but not specific sounds / small file size / easy manipulation / some information lost
SLIDE 11: Audio Representations || Raw waveforms /  / X-axis represents time / Y-axis represents displacement of air molecules / The oscillation frequency represents pitch / Wave shape represents timbre
SLIDE 12: Audio Representations || Spectrogram  /  / Commonly used in style transfer model architectures / X-axis represents time / Y-axis represents frequency / Color intensity represents strength of the frequency / Calculated from raw waveforms using Short Time Fourier Transform
SLIDE 13: Short Time Fourier Transform || Fourier Transform: Tells us what a signal is made out of /  / Signals can be made out of individual sinusoids with properties like their frequency, amplitude, and position in the signal. /  / It answers the questions: What frequencies are inside this signal
SLIDE 14: Short Time Fourier Transform || f(t): Audio signal / e-iωt:  Sinusoidal wave || Fourier Transform: || Instead of telling you amplitude over time, the output of fourier transform tells you strength at each frequency
SLIDE 15: Short Time Fourier Transform || Problem || The Fourier Transform looks at the entire signal at once, so it tells you which frequencies exist, but not when they appear
SLIDE 16: Short Time Fourier Transform || Solution || The Short Time Fourier Transform fixes this with one simple idea: instead of looking at the entire signal at once, you only look at a small slice at a time.
SLIDE 17: The Realism Problem || Even if a model preserves the right structure it can still sound bad. Audio is unforgiving with lots of intertwined components. Individual components that sound good may sound bad when played together.

SLIDE 29: Variational Autoencoders (VAES) || Compress input into latent space (encoder), reconstruct (decoder) / Naive implementation prone to posterior collapse / Incredibly important to style-transfer as in extracts a latent representation
SLIDE 30: RAVE || Uses Convolutional layers instead of RNN / Uses spectral losses instead of only raw sample-wise reconstruction. / Two stage training procedure: / Trains encoder and decoder to learn compact latent representation / Trains only decoder with GAN discriminator  / Enables style transfer  / Encode from one domain, decode from another
